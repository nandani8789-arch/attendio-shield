Q1. Compulsory Mechanical Concepts (Answer ALL 7 parts): 7 × 2 = 14 Marks
(a) State the First Law of Thermodynamics for an open system undergoing a steady flow process. Write the Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE).
(b) State Kelvin-Planck and Clausius statements of the Second Law of Thermodynamics and prove their equivalence.
(c) What is dryness fraction (steam quality)? How does superheating affect the thermal efficiency of a Rankine cycle?
(d) Differentiate between Streamline, Streakline, and Pathline in steady and unsteady fluid flow.
(e) State Bernoulli's theorem along a streamline and write down its key underlying assumptions.
(f) Define Dynamic Viscosity and Kinematic Viscosity. State Newton's Law of Viscosity.
(g) What is Boundary Layer Separation? Mention two engineering methods to delay boundary layer separation over aerodynamic surfaces.
Q2. Steam Power Cycles (Rankine & Reheat): 8 + 6 = 14 Marks
(a) In a steam power plant operating on an ideal Rankine cycle with reheat, steam enters the high-pressure turbine at 150 bar, 550°C and expands to 40 bar. It is then reheated at constant pressure to 550°C before expanding in the low-pressure turbine to a condenser pressure of 0.1 bar. The net power output of the plant is 120 MW.
Determine:
  • Thermal efficiency of the reheat cycle.
  • Mass flow rate of steam (kg/s).
  • Specific steam consumption (SSC in kg/kWh).
(b) Draw the T-s and h-s (Mollier) diagrams for the reheat Rankine cycle and show the effect of boiler pressure elevation.
Q3. Fluid Kinematics & Continuity: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) The velocity potential function for a two-dimensional fluid flow is given by:
φ = x^2 - y^2 + 3xy.
  • Check whether the flow is irrotational and satisfies the continuity equation.
  • Find the corresponding Stream Function ψ.
  • Compute the velocity magnitude and discharge at point P(2, 3).
(b) Derive the three-dimensional differential Continuity Equation in Cartesian coordinates for unsteady, compressible fluid flow. Deduce its simplified form for steady incompressible flow.
Q4. Fluid Dynamics & Flow Measurement: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) Derive the expression for the theoretical and actual discharge through a horizontal Venturimeter. Explain why the diverging cone has a smaller angle (5° to 7°) compared to the converging cone (20° to 22°).
(b) A pipeline carrying crude oil (specific gravity = 0.85) has inlet and throat diameters of 25 cm and 12.5 cm respectively. A differential U-tube mercury manometer connects the inlet and throat and indicates a mercury deflection of 22 cm. If coefficient of discharge C_d = 0.98, calculate the volumetric flow rate in liters/second.
Q5. Viscous Flow in Pipes (Poiseuille Flow): 8 + 6 = 14 Marks
(a) Derive the Hagen-Poiseuille Equation for laminar viscous flow through a circular pipe of radius R:
ΔP = (32 * μ * u_avg * L) / D^2.
Prove that the maximum velocity at the centerline is twice the average cross-sectional velocity.
(b) Explain the Darcy-Weisbach equation for frictional head loss in pipes. Sketch the Moody chart and explain the significance of relative roughness (ε/D) in turbulent flow.
