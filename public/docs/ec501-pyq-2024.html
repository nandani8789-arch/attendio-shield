Q1. Compulsory Fundamentals Section (Answer ALL 7 parts): 7 × 2 = 14 Marks
(a) Differentiate between Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT) and Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT).
(b) State the Twiddle Factor W_N in DFT and prove its periodicity and symmetry properties.
(c) How many complex multiplications and complex additions are needed to compute an N-point DFT directly vs using Radix-2 FFT?
(d) What is Frequency Warping in the Bilinear Transformation Method? How is pre-warping applied to overcome it?
(e) Why are FIR digital filters always inherently stable compared to IIR filters?
(f) State the Gibbs Phenomenon in Fourier series design of FIR filters.
(g) What is the purpose of down-sampling (decimation) and up-sampling (interpolation) in Multirate DSP?
Q2. Radix-2 Fast Fourier Transform Algorithms: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) Derive the Decimation-in-Time (DIT) Radix-2 FFT algorithm. Given input sequence x(n) = [1, 2, 3, 4, 4, 3, 2, 1], compute its 8-point DFT by drawing the complete butterfly diagram with bit-reversed indices.
(b) Explain the Decimation-in-Frequency (DIF) Radix-2 FFT formulation and contrast it with DIT in terms of input ordering and output ordering.
Q3. Circular Convolution & Overlap-Save / Add Methods: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) Compute the circular convolution of two finite-duration sequences x1(n) = [2, 1, 2, 1] and x2(n) = [1, 2, 3, 4] using:
  • Matrix Multiplication method
  • DFT and IDFT property method
(b) Explain the Overlap-Save and Overlap-Add methods for linear filtering of long data streams with block diagrams and index ranges.
Q4. IIR Filter Design (Butterworth & Chebyshev): 8 + 6 = 14 Marks
(a) Design an analog and equivalent digital Butterworth low-pass filter using Bilinear Transformation to meet the specifications:
Passband ripple ≤ 1.5 dB at Ωp = 0.2π
Stopband attenuation ≥ 10 dB at Ωs = 0.3π
Determine order N, cutoff frequency, and transfer function H(z).
(b) Compare Butterworth filters with Chebyshev Type-I and Type-II filters regarding magnitude response, passband ripple, and transition band roll-off.
Q5. FIR Filter Design using Windows: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) Design an ideal linear-phase Low-Pass FIR Filter with cutoff frequency ωc = π/4 rad/sec with length M = 7 using a Hamming Window. Calculate filter coefficients h(n) and write the system function H(z).
(b) Explain the condition for Linear Phase in FIR digital filters. Classify Type-1, Type-2, Type-3, and Type-4 linear-phase FIR systems with symmetry conditions.
Q6. Digital Filter Realization Structures: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) Realize the following IIR transfer function in Direct Form-I, Direct Form-II, Cascade Form, and Parallel Form:
H(z) = (1 + 2z^-1 + z^-2) / [ (1 - 0.75z^-1 + 0.125z^-2) * (1 - 0.5z^-1) ]
(b) Realize a Linear Phase FIR system function in Direct Form and Linear Phase Lattice Form:
H(z) = 1 + (1/2)z^-1 + (1/3)z^-2 + (1/2)z^-3 + z^-4
Q7. Finite Word-Length Effects: 7 + 7 = 14 Marks
(a) Explain the effect of coefficient quantization on the pole locations of high-order IIR filters. Why is second-order biquad cascading preferred over high-order direct structures?
(b) Describe Limit Cycle Oscillations in digital filters: Zero-Input Limit Cycles (due to quantization) and Overflow Limit Cycles. How does saturation arithmetic eliminate overflow oscillations?
